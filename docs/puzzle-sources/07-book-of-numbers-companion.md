# Source 07 — The Book of Numbers: Complete Companion Guide

Provenance: user-supplied companion guide to Shakuntala Devi's *The Book of Numbers* (number-systems history, mathematician anecdotes, and reference tables — distinct from the puzzle-and-solution catalog already covered in files 05/06). Added to the reference library on 2026-08-03.

**This is mostly background material, not a puzzle catalog** — same role as `04-tabletop-compendium-background.md`: useful for flavor text, worked-example tip cards, and trivia, but the bulk of it (number-system history, etymology, mathematician biographies, unit-conversion tables, metric prefixes) has no single-answer question to extract and isn't puzzle content on its own. Below is what's actually usable, split from what's pure background.

## Genuine puzzle content (usable, with the usual independent-verification caveat)

- **The Broken Necklace** (a pearl necklace splits into 1/3, 1/5, 1/6, 1/10 fractions + 6 remaining pearls) — **already catalogued** as source 05 §B1 with the same worked answer (**30 pearls**). This document is a duplicate copy of the same puzzle from the same "Lilavati" chapter — no new information, just confirms the answer via an independent restatement.
- **The Beaming-Eyed Maiden reverse-arithmetic chain** (×3, +3/4, ÷7, −1/3, square, −52, √, +8, ÷10 → 2) — **already catalogued** as source 05 §B2, answer **28**. Also a duplicate, confirms the working step-by-step (this copy's Step 7 spells out the algebra — "multiply by 3/2" — slightly more explicitly than the other handbook's version, useful if adapting this into a hint-ladder step).
- **The "Proof That 1 = 2" Fallacy** — *new puzzle candidate.* Setup: a 6-step algebraic "proof" starting from a=b that concludes 1=2 (multiply by a, subtract b², factor, cancel (a−b), substitute, divide by b). Find the invalid step. **Validated answer: the error is in the cancellation step** — dividing both sides by (a−b) is illegal because a=b makes a−b=0, and division by zero is undefined. Good "Logic & Knowledge" or "Patterns & Numbers" MC puzzle: ask which step introduces the error, with the other steps as distractors. Clean, self-contained, single correct answer, no dispute risk.
- **Sessa and the Grains of Wheat / doubling on a 64-square chessboard** — Setup: 1 grain on square 1, doubling each square, 64 squares total. Validated answer: **2⁶⁴−1 = 18,446,744,073,709,551,615 grains**. Well-known ("wheat and chessboard problem") but clean and computable — usable as an "Algorithms & Optimization" or exponential-growth puzzle, e.g. asking for the total or for a specific square's grain count, or reframing around the volume/bushels comparison the source gives (over 320 billion bushels).
- **Prime Years of the 20th Century** — trivia fact: only 7 years in the 1900s have a prime year-number (**1951, 1973, 1979, 1987, 1993, 1997, 1999**). Could support a "how many/which of these" number-theory MC question — straightforward to independently verify (just test primality of each candidate year).
- **Perfect numbers** (28 = 1+2+4+7+14) and **amicable numbers** (220 & 284) — short, verifiable number-theory facts, usable as "identify/verify the number-theory property" puzzles. Note the source itself flags that some editions have a typo calling the amicable pair "220 and 224" — this file's own text already corrects it to 220/284; just don't propagate the typo if quoting the source loosely.
- **Casting Out Nines** (digital-root verification trick for multiplication) — not a puzzle with a single answer, but a good **worked-example tip card** candidate (Step 4 of the rotation playbook) — teaches a genuinely reusable verification technique, matches the site's existing tip-card format (short explanation + compact worked example).

## Background only — not puzzle content

Everything else in this document is explanatory/historical background, not adaptable into a single-answer MC puzzle: number-system foundations (what is a number/numeral/digit), Roman numeral history, the invention of zero, mathematical symbol etymology (+, −, ÷, =, √), the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic explanation, algorithm-vs-logarithm definitions, the Farey sequence and Fibonacci sequence background, magic squares (including the Khajuraho diabolic square — could theoretically become a "verify this magic square" puzzle if ever needed, but it's presented as an illustration, not a puzzle to solve), metric-vs-English measurement history, mathematician anecdotes (Bhaskaracharya/Lilavati backstory, Euclid, Hypatia, Omar Khayyam, Descartes, Newton's absentmindedness, Einstein's homework story, Ramanujan/Hardy, the Julian/Gregorian calendar shift), and the reference tables at the end (divisibility rules, decimal equivalents of fractions, metric prefixes, unit-conversion factors, Earth's physical constants).

Some of this background is good **flavor text or worked-example material** for tip cards (divisibility rules, the Fibonacci/Pascal's-triangle connection, the Fahrenheit/Celsius conversion formula already partially used via puzzle #31 "Blow Hot Blow Cold" in source 06) but none of it is itself a puzzle with a question/options/answer to adapt.
